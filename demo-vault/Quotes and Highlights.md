# Quotes and Highlights

How Ivory Garden handles blockquotes, nested quotes, and text highlights.

## Blockquotes

> This is a blockquote. The left border uses the warm taupe border color, and the text is slightly muted compared to body text. Blockquotes are useful for pulling out a passage from another source.

> This is a longer blockquote that wraps across multiple lines. Notice how the left border stays consistent and the text maintains comfortable line height. The muted text color helps the quote feel distinct from the body without disappearing entirely.

## Nested quotes

> Outer quote starts here.
>
> > Inner quote is indented further and uses a slightly different border color.
> >
> > The nesting is visually clear without being heavy.
>
> Back to the outer quote.

## Blockquote with formatting

> **Bold quote.** You can use *italic*, `inline code`, [links](Welcome), and ==highlights== inside blockquotes.
>
> Multiple paragraphs work too — just use a blank line and a `>` prefix.

## Highlights

The signature gold glow is Ivory Garden's defining feature. Highlights use muted gold (`#C49A3C` at 35% opacity) instead of the default yellow.

==This is a highlighted passage.== Notice how the gold tint is warm but not aggressive — it draws the eye without shouting.

Here's a paragraph with ==a highlighted phrase== in the middle, followed by more text. The highlight integrates with the flow rather than breaking it.

==You can highlight entire sentences or even multiple sentences. The gold tint stays consistent across line breaks and paragraph boundaries.==

## Blockquote + highlight combination

> ==A highlighted passage inside a blockquote.== The combination is rare but works — the gold tint sits on top of the muted quote background, creating a layered effect.

## Inline highlights in context

When you read a long passage, you might want to ==mark key phrases for later review==. The gold tint is distinctive enough to find with a quick scan, but subtle enough not to break your reading flow. This is ==particularly useful for research notes== where you're pulling out citations from multiple sources.
